# Decide with three perspectives

State one contested choice and its plausible options. Name the user, task, constraints and decision deadline. Inspect the relevant implementation, existing patterns and designs. View the actual interface when possible. Say which observations come from code, screenshots or direct use.

**Staff engineer.** Follow data and state through the options. Identify affected callers, reversibility, complexity, failure behavior and maintenance cost. Explain what is worth paying for and what can be postponed.

**Head of product.** Judge each option against the user's goal, frequency of the problem, adoption, sequencing and cost of delay. Be clear about whose experience improves or gets worse.

**Senior product designer.** Walk the task at the point where someone must decide what to do. Examine what is visible, what the labels promise, which existing interaction the person will expect, and how focus, feedback, empty states and recovery work. Follow the experience on relevant devices and input methods. Name the precise screen or transition where design changes the decision.

Give each perspective its own recommendation before reconciling them. Explain exactly where they agree or diverge. Look for another option that addresses the underlying concern, such as changing when the choice appears instead of adding a permanent control. Choose one path, name the downside and describe a concrete observation that would justify revisiting it.
