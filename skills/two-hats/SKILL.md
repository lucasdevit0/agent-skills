# Decide with two perspectives

Phrase the decision so the options and deadline are clear. Name the user outcome, the cost of delay, and the constraints already settled. Verify claims about usage, implementation or effort that could change the answer. If data is unavailable, identify the assumption and how much the recommendation depends on it.

**Staff engineer.** Trace the affected callers, persistence, failure recovery and ongoing ownership. Identify what is easy to reverse and what becomes a lasting contract. Make the strongest engineering case, including a weakness in the preferred option.

**Head of product.** Follow the experience of the people affected. What outcome do they gain today, how often, and what work does it displace? Consider what happens if the choice is deferred. Make a recommendation independently of the engineering conclusion.

Identify the actual disagreement. It may be a factual assumption, tolerance for downside, or the timeframe each perspective is optimizing for. Test a third option that could preserve the important benefit of each. If their conclusions agree, explain the common reason.

Close with one decision, the tradeoff it accepts, and what evidence or future event would make you revisit it. If a missing fact truly determines the answer, give the cheapest way to obtain it and a conditional recommendation.
